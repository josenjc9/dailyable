DECISION METHOD

Observe first.
Ask with care.
Change one thing at a time.

This page helps a supporter move from a signal to a low-risk action without guessing a diagnosis. Every step names the evidence needed, the question to ask, and what must be recorded next.

01 · DECISION PATH

Six decisions, each with a stop rule.

Move down the table only when the previous row is clear. A missing answer is a reason to ask, not a reason to invent.

DecisionEvidence to useAsk / doStop or continue
01SafetyWhat the person says now; explicit danger only.Are you in immediate danger, or do you need someone with you now?Yes → fixed human safety route. Do not continue the routine analysis.
02Observable changeDated check-ins and the person’s own earlier pattern.What changed, when did it start, and what is still the same?State the fact without a cause, label, score, or comparison with others.
03ContextSleep, food and water, instructions, sensory load, environment, relationships.What was different around the time this became harder?Keep more than one possible explanation until the person confirms one.
04FunctionThe exact point where participation stops or costs more effort.What would make the next two hours easier?Name one task, step, place, cue, tool, pace, or person.
05Small support trialThe person’s choice and the lowest-risk reversible option.Try one change: shorten, pause, show, move, remind, or connect.Agree on who does what and when to review. Do not change three things at once.
06Observed resultWhat became easier, stayed the same, or became harder after the trial.Did this help enough to repeat, adjust, stop, or ask a professional?Record the result and next review date. An outcome closes the loop.

02 · ASK WITHOUT LEADING

Questions that open context instead of assigning meaning.

Start gently

Let the person set the frame

  • What feels most different today?
  • Where would you like me to start?
  • Do you want ideas, company, or just someone to listen?
Find the context

Ask around the change

  • What happened just before this became harder?
  • Was the instruction, place, noise, timing, or person different?
  • How were sleep, food, water, and physical comfort?
Find the function

Locate the difficult step

  • Which part takes the most effort right now?
  • Is starting, continuing, switching, or finishing the hard part?
  • What would make the next two hours easier?
Confirm permission

Keep the choice visible

  • Would you like me to help with that one step?
  • Who, if anyone, may receive this summary?
  • When should we check whether it helped?

03 · ALEX EXAMPLE

From three signals to one reversible trial.

OBSERVEDThree lower-energy check-ins; interrupted sleep; morning meal routine paused.No cause assigned.
ASKEDWhat changed first, and which part makes starting the morning hardest?Alex says preparing food feels like too many steps.
TRIEDChoose one ready-to-eat breakfast for tomorrow; Jordan checks in at 10:00.One change, agreed by Alex, easy to stop.
RECORDDid starting the morning take less effort? Repeat, adjust, or stop.The result matters more than the theory.

04 · CLOSE THE LOOP

A useful record answers five things.

  1. 1
    Observable fact

    What changed, when, and for how long?

  2. 2
    Person’s own words

    What did they say was difficult or useful?

  3. 3
    One agreed action

    Who will do exactly what?

  4. 4
    Observed result

    Easier, unchanged, harder, or not tried?

  5. 5
    Next review

    Date, owner, and whether to repeat, adjust, stop, or escalate.

Authority boundary

Supporters improve participation. They do not diagnose.

Fixed rules route immediate danger. Participants choose sharing and their support goals. Supporters carry out agreed support decisions. Qualified professionals make clinical decisions.
